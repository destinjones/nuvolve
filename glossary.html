Pest control glossary

The words you'll hear from inspectors, distributors and other operators, in plain English.

Active ingredient (AI)
The chemical in a pesticide that controls the pest. The label gives it as a percentage; the rest is inert ingredients.
Applicator
A person licensed by the state to apply pesticides. States use different titles: certified applicator, technician, apprentice, operator.
Bait station
A tamper-resistant container holding rodent or termite bait, usually numbered and mapped so each one is checked on every visit.
Callback
A return visit because the pest came back between scheduled services. Often free within a re-treat window, and a key quality number for a company.
CEU (continuing education unit)
Training hours a licensed applicator must earn to renew their license. Each state sets how many and in which categories.
Crack and crevice treatment
Applying a small amount of pesticide into cracks and gaps where pests hide, rather than onto open surfaces.
10DLC
The registration U.S. carriers require for businesses that send texts from regular 10-digit phone numbers. Unregistered business texts get blocked.
EPA registration number
The number the U.S. EPA assigns to each registered pesticide product, printed on its label as "EPA Reg. No.". Most state records require it.
Exclusion
Sealing the gaps pests and wildlife use to get into a building, such as door sweeps, vent screens and sealed utility openings.
FIFRA
The Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act, the federal law that governs how pesticides are registered, sold, labeled and used.
Fumigation
Treating a whole structure with a gas pesticide under a tarp or seal, used for drywood termites and bed bugs. Requires special licensing and logs.
IGR (insect growth regulator)
A product that stops insects from developing or reproducing, rather than killing adults directly.
IPM (integrated pest management)
Controlling pests with inspection, monitoring, sanitation, exclusion and targeted treatment, using pesticides only as needed. Standard in commercial accounts.
Label
The legal instructions on a pesticide product. "The label is the law": using a product differently from its label is a violation.
Perimeter treatment
A band of pesticide applied around the outside of a building to stop pests getting in.
PPE (personal protective equipment)
Gloves, eye protection, respirators and other gear the product label requires the applicator to wear.
Pre-treat (pre-construction treatment)
A termite treatment applied to the soil or framing while a building is under construction.
Re-entry interval (REI)
How long people or pets must stay out of a treated area after an application, as set on the product label.
Recurring service (plan)
A service the customer pays for on a schedule, such as quarterly pest control. Most of a pest company's value is in its recurring revenue.
RUP (restricted use pesticide)
A pesticide that may only be applied by, or under the direct supervision of, a certified applicator, with extra recordkeeping.
SDS (safety data sheet)
The manufacturer's document describing a product's hazards, handling and first aid. Must be available to workers.
Structural pest control
Pest control in and around buildings, as opposed to agricultural pest control. Usually licensed by a state agriculture department or a structural pest control board.
Termite warranty (bond)
A contract promising re-treatment, and sometimes repairs, if termites return. Usually renewed every year for a fee.
Termiticide
A pesticide made to kill or repel termites, applied as a liquid barrier, foam, or bait.
WDI / WDO inspection
A wood-destroying insect (or organism) inspection, usually for a real estate sale. Many lenders require a report on a specific form, such as NPMA-33.
