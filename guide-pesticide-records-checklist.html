The pesticide application record checklist (any state)

By Destin Jones, founder of Nuvolve, who builds it for owners who still run a route · Published November 1, 2026

Every state writes its own rule, but they ask for nearly the same things. If every ticket captures the list below, you'll be close to compliant almost anywhere. Then check your own state's rule for the extras.

The fields almost every state asks for

  • Customer and service address, including the billing name when it differs
  • Date of the application (some states also want the time)
  • Product name and EPA registration number for every pesticide used
  • Amount applied, and for concentrates the mix rate or percentage of active ingredient
  • Target pest or the reason for the treatment
  • Where on the site it was applied (interior, exterior perimeter, crawlspace, bait stations)
  • Applicator's name and license number, plus the supervisor's when a trainee applied
  • The company's business license number

Extras some states add

  • Termite work: square or linear footage treated, diagrams, tank counts and times for pre-treats (Texas, for example)
  • Monthly reports to a county or state agency (California, for example)
  • Customer notices before or after treatment, with set wording
  • Restricted use pesticides: extra federal and state recordkeeping
  • How long to keep records: commonly two or three years, but it varies by state and type of work

Keep them without a clipboard

  1. Set up your products once, with EPA registration numbers and default mix rates.
  2. Load each truck's stock, so the technician picks products from what's actually on board.
  3. Make the record part of finishing the stop, not a separate form, so it can't be skipped.
  4. Keep license numbers on each technician's profile, with expiry warnings.
  5. Be able to export any date range in minutes when the inspector asks.

That's how Nuvolve does it: the pesticide record is written as the technician finishes the stop, from the products on their truck.
