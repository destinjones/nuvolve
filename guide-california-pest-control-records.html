California structural pest control records and monthly use reports

By Destin Jones, founder of Nuvolve, who builds it for owners who still run a route · Published November 1, 2026

California has some of the strictest pesticide recordkeeping in the country. A structural pest control company answers to the Structural Pest Control Board, the Department of Pesticide Regulation and the county agricultural commissioner. Here are the three things that trip companies up most.

The short version: keep inspection reports, contracts, notices and pesticide use records for at least three years; send a monthly pesticide use report to the county agricultural commissioner by the 10th of the following month; and give the owner and tenant the required written notice before you treat.

1. Keep records for three years

Business and Professions Code §8652 makes it grounds for discipline if a registered company fails to keep its inspection reports, field notes, contracts, documents, notices of work completed and records (other than financial records) for at least three years after the work. Pesticide use records also have a three-year requirement under DPR's regulations, and county inspectors check for it during headquarters inspections. Records must be available at your headquarters during business hours (Food and Agricultural Code §15205).

2. Send the monthly pesticide use report

Structural pest control companies report their pesticide use to the agricultural commissioner of each county they worked in, each month, generally by the 10th day of the following month. Counties publish the forms and the submission method, and some accept electronic reporting; check with each county you work in.

3. Give the notice before you treat

Business and Professions Code §8538 requires a registered company to give the owner (or owner's agent) and tenant clear written notice that names the pest to be controlled, the pesticides and active ingredients to be used, and the state-required "CAUTION — PESTICIDES ARE TOXIC CHEMICALS" statement, with the rest of the wording the law prescribes.
